## A recovery narrative built on a shrinking number

Every few weeks through 2026, China's National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) has published a retail sales figure, and every few weeks the same headline has followed: consumption is recovering. It's not wrong, exactly. But read the NBS releases themselves rather than the headlines built on top of them, and the growth rate this "recovery" describes has been quietly narrowing all year, and it is carried almost entirely by one category of spending rather than the broad-based rebound the framing implies.

In the first quarter of 2026, retail sales of consumer goods rose 2.4% year on year to 12.77 trillion yuan, a pace the NBS itself described as 0.7 percentage points faster than the fourth quarter of 2025 — the basis for the recovery framing in the first place ([NBS, via Xinhua, 16 April 2026](https://english.news.cn/20260416/7132db705a46496480d1afb3572af7fd/c.html)). By the first half of the year, the NBS began reporting a new headline figure — total retail sales of goods *and* services combined — which grew 2.7% year on year ([NBS, via China SCIO, 15 July 2026](http://english.scio.gov.cn/pressroom/2026-07/15/content_118600022.html)). By the seven-month mark through July, that same combined figure had eased slightly to 2.6% ([NBS, via China SCIO, 17 August 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/statistics/202608/17/content_WS6a82b953c6d00ca5f9a0ca39.html)). None of these numbers describe acceleration. They describe a consumption trend that improved modestly off a weak 2025 base, then plateaued.

## The category split the headline number hides

The more informative number sits one level down. Across every release NBS has published in 2026, the same pattern holds: retail sales of *goods* have grown at roughly 1.1% to 2.2% year on year, while retail sales of *services* have consistently grown at 5% to 5.5% ([NBS, H1 2026 release, 15 July 2026](http://english.scio.gov.cn/pressroom/2026-07/15/content_118600022.html); [NBS, seven-month release, 17 August 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/statistics/202608/17/content_WS6a82b953c6d00ca5f9a0ca39.html)). NBS chief statistician Yuan Yan made the pattern explicit in the August release, noting that "retail sales of services have consistently grown faster than retail sales of goods over the past three years, with a clear shift in the consumption market from a focus solely on goods to placing greater emphasis on both goods and services."

This is not a subtle distinction for anyone selling physical products into China. A consumer market where services spending is growing two to five times faster than goods spending is not the same market a decade of "China retail growth" headlines trained international brands to expect. The NBS data specifically calls out communication and information services, tourism-related leasing and consulting, and cultural and recreational services as the fastest-growing components within that services figure — categories that, notably, sit closer to experience and connectivity spending than to the discretionary goods purchases that dominated China consumption narratives through the 2010s.

## What NBS's own officials are saying about the softness

It's worth taking seriously that NBS's own deputy head, Mao Shengyong, has repeatedly qualified the growth figures at press conferences rather than simply presenting them as unambiguous good news. At the April briefing, he noted that "residents' consumption capacity and willingness need further enhancement, and the supply of high-quality goods and services still cannot fully meet the diversified consumption demands" ([NBS, via China's State Council, 16 April 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/statistics/202604/16/content_WS69e04aaac6d00ca5f9a0a72d.html)). This is an unusually direct acknowledgment, in an official statistics briefing, that the recovery narrative has real limits — willingness to spend, not just capacity to spend, remains a constraint the government is naming publicly rather than glossing over.

One place the data does show genuine strength worth separating from the broader softness: new energy vehicle (NEV) retail penetration crossed 60% and held there for multiple consecutive months through mid-2026, reaching 62.8% in June ([NBS, H1 2026 release, 15 July 2026](http://english.scio.gov.cn/pressroom/2026-07/15/content_118600022.html)). That is a specific, structural, category-level success story — not evidence that consumer spending broadly has turned a corner, but a genuine signal that a major purchase category has shifted decisively.

## Online, rural, and the shape of where growth actually concentrates

Two further patterns from the NBS data are worth noting for anyone planning market entry or channel strategy. First, online retail sales of goods and services have grown consistently faster than the headline figure throughout 2026 -- 5.9% to 9.2% year on year across different releases -- meaning e-commerce channels have captured a disproportionate share of whatever growth exists ([NBS, various 2026 monthly releases](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/)). Second, rural retail sales have outpaced urban retail sales in every release this year, a genuinely persistent pattern rather than a one-off: rural retail sales of consumer goods rose 2.6% year on year through the first five months of 2026, 1.4 percentage points faster than urban areas, and 2.4% through the first seven months, 1.3 points faster than urban -- both figures stated directly in NBS's own official releases ([NBS, via China's State Council, 16 June 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/statistics/202606/16/content_WS6a30e418c6d00ca5f9a0b9db.html); [Xinhua, reporting a State Council Information Office press conference on county-level consumption, 18 August 2026](https://english.news.cn/20260818/71b74d4c9f5343da8aa18bd0d8fc38e5/c.html)).

County- and township-level markets specifically are large enough to matter on their own terms: officials at an 18 August 2026 State Council Information Office press conference put county- and township-level retail sales at "nearly 40 percent" of the national total, with lower-tier markets more broadly -- third-tier cities, counties, townships, and rural areas together -- accounting for roughly 70% of China's population and 60% of total retail sales of consumer goods, according to the same briefing ([Xinhua, 18 August 2026](https://english.news.cn/20260818/71b74d4c9f5343da8aa18bd0d8fc38e5/c.html); corroborated independently by [South China Morning Post, citing the Ministry of Commerce's separate H1 2026 figure of 39.2%, 19 August 2026](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3364437/beijing-eyes-rural-markets-local-town-centres-tackle-national-consumption-woes)). A single vendor-style aggregator had earlier cited a more precise 39.7% figure for the January-April period specifically, but since that specific number could not be independently corroborated against a second source, it has been dropped in favour of the "nearly 40%" figure that two independent outlets confirm directly from official briefings.

Neither of these patterns is new to China-market analysts. But taken together with the goods-versus-services divergence, they describe a consumption recovery that is real but structurally narrow: concentrated in services rather than goods, in online rather than offline channels, and modestly stronger in lower-tier markets than the major cities that dominate most brand strategy conversations.

## A reporting change that's easy to miss, and why it matters

One further detail is worth flagging specifically because it's easy to miss and changes how year-over-year comparisons should be read. NBS included its combined goods-and-services retail sales indicator in its monthly data package for the first time with the January-May 2026 release, spokesperson Fu Linghui confirmed directly, explaining the change as reflecting "the growing importance of services in China's consumption landscape" ([NBS, via China's State Council, 16 June 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/statistics/202606/16/content_WS6a30e418c6d00ca5f9a0b9db.html)). Before that point, the headline retail sales figure most commonly cited in international coverage measured goods alone, systematically understating the services-led growth this article has focused on. Anyone comparing 2026 "retail sales" figures against 2025 or earlier headline numbers should check which indicator — goods only, or the new combined goods-and-services figure — is actually being compared, since the two describe meaningfully different things and the switch happened mid-year.

The month-by-month goods-only figures NBS has published throughout 2026 tell a consistent story on their own: 2.8% year-on-year growth in the January-February data, 2.4% for Q1, 1.9% through April, 1.4% through May — a gently decelerating trend across the first half of the year before the combined goods-and-services measure took over as NBS's preferred headline ([NBS monthly releases, February-June 2026](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/)). That deceleration in the goods-only series is arguably the more honest year-over-year comparison for anyone tracking momentum specifically in physical product categories, since it isn't flattered by the stronger services figures the newer combined indicator folds in.

## What this means for strategy

For a business planning a China consumption strategy off the "recovery" headline alone, the NBS data itself argues for a more specific read. A goods-focused brand entering or expanding in China in late 2026 is competing in a segment growing at roughly a third the pace of services — and should size expectations accordingly rather than assuming the aggregate 2.6-2.7% figure applies evenly. A brand with a services or experience component — travel-adjacent, cultural, communications-linked — is operating in the genuinely stronger part of the market, and the NBS data gives a defensible basis for more optimistic planning there specifically.

The reasonable conclusion from a full year of NBS releases is not that China's consumer recovery is a fiction — the data plainly shows real, sustained growth, especially in services. It is that "recovery" is doing a lot of work as a single word to describe a consumption pattern that NBS's own officials have been careful, in their own briefings, not to overstate.
